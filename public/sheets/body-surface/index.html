Body surface area, two ways

The area of a person’s skin, estimated from weight and height. One straight line reads it by two formulas at once: the one built on nine bodies in 1916, and the one refitted from all forty-two the same paper reported.

For reading, not for dosing. The values are the two formulas solved exactly for the line you have drawn.

Directions

  1. Set the left ring at the weight.
  2. Set the right ring at the height.
  3. Read the surface area where the line crosses the pair of middle scales: the left-hand figures for Shuter and Aslani, the right-hand figures for Du Bois.

Remarks

In 1916 D. Du Bois and E. F. Du Bois fitted a formula for skin area to height and weight. It is still the standard one, used, in the words of a 2000 reappraisal, “widely to normalise physiological parameters.” The same reappraisal pointed out what few of its users know: the fit rests on nine subjects, measured by encasing them in moulds. B. Shuter and A. Aslani refitted the model to all forty-two subjects the original paper reported, using measurements of body parts that Du Bois and Du Bois had left out of their fit, and got different constants.

A chart of this kind is a good place to compare them, because on it a formula has a position. Each formula is a power law, a constant times weight to one power and height to another. Taking logarithms turns that into a sum, and a sum is what three parallel scales compute. Where the answer scale stands between the outer two is fixed by nothing but the ratio of the two powers. Du Bois’s 0.425 to 0.725 puts its scale at one place. Shuter and Aslani’s 0.441 to 0.655 puts theirs a little to the left. The thin gap between the two lines is the whole of the disagreement about how weight and height trade off, drawn to scale.

It is a small gap. Anywhere on this sheet the two answers differ by at most about 4 percent, a figure reached only in the extreme corner, and over most of it by one to three. The disagreement has a direction, though. The two agree along a band of stocky builds, from about 65 kg at 140 cm to 150 kg at 168 cm. Away from that band, toward the tall and light, the refit gives less skin than Du Bois, and the gap is widest at the tall, light corner of the chart. Lower the left ring, raise the right one, and watch the difference grow.

Formulas

Du Bois: A = 0.007184 × W0.425 × H0.725. Shuter and Aslani: A = 0.00949 × W0.441 × H0.655. A in square metres, W in kilograms, H in centimetres. Shuter and Aslani give their constant as 94.9 for an area in square centimetres. The scales are log W, log H, and log A.
